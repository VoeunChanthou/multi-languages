How to read the pronunciation Spanish is spelled the way it sounds
CAPSSay this syllable louder: GRAH-see-ahs
aha, as in “father”
ehe, as in “bed”
eei, as in “see”
oho, as in “go” (short, pure)
oou, as in “food”
hj and ge/gi: a breathy h, as in “hat”
yll and y: as in “yes”
nyñ: as in “canyon”
sz, ce, ci: as in “sun” (Spain: “th”)
rsingle r: a quick tap, like the tt in “butter”
rrrr and r at the start: a rolled r
kqu, and c before a/o/u
vv: soft, close to b
—h is always silent: hola = OH-lah
eyeay / hay: rhymes with “my”

Tip: Spanish vowels never change. Keep them short and clear. When you see two forms like cansado / cansada, a man says the first and a woman says the second. Pronunciation here follows Latin American Spanish, which is understood everywhere.
