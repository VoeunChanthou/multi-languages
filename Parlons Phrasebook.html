How to read the pronunciation Many final letters are silent
CAPSLean lightly on the last syllable of a phrase: bohn-ZHOOR
aha, as in “father”
ehè, ê, ai: as in “bed”
ayé, er, ez: as in “say”, but shorter
eei: as in “see”
oho, au, eau: as in “go”, short
oo before a spoken consonant: as in “lot” (bonne = bonn)
ooou: as in “food”
ewu: say “ee” with rounded lips (tu = tew)
uhe, eu: as in “the” (le = luh)
wahoi: as in “wah” (moi = mwah)
zhj, ge, gi: the s in “measure”
shch: as in “shoe”
nygn: as in “canyon”
rr: soft and throaty, at the back of the mouth
ahn ohn anNasal vowels: let air out through the nose and don’t fully say the n
—Final letters are usually silent: petit = puh-TEE

Tip: French joins words together. A silent final letter is often spoken when the next word starts with a vowel: les amis = lay-zah-MEE. When you see two forms like fatigué / fatiguée, a man writes the first and a woman the second; they usually sound the same. Use vous with strangers and tu with friends.
